The problem we own

Two broken options, and a bill at the end of both

If you are a hardware team with one to three engineers and no EM specialist, neither of the existing paths was built for you.

Enterprise CEM is priced for primes

HFSS, CST, COMSOL, and Sigrity run five to six figures a year per seat, plus HPC infrastructure you have to run yourself. They are built around aerospace workflows: steep learning curves, heavy setup, and far more solver than a board-level or enclosure problem needs.

The consulting route is opaque

EMC labs and boutique consultants are slow to quote, cagey about price, and describe their work in language a non-RF founder cannot evaluate. You cannot budget for a number nobody will give you.

So the test lab tells you first

The result is that most teams learn they had an EMI problem from a failed submission — a late, expensive fire drill with a respin attached, instead of a planned step in the schedule with a known cost.

What you actually get

A report that names the part and the fix

The deliverable is not a folder of S-parameters and field plots for someone else to interpret. It is a ranked list of what is wrong, how confident we are, and the specific change to make.

  • Pass / Risk / Fail on every item, with the margin in dB
  • Reference designators and coordinates, not abstractions
  • The raw simulation data attached underneath, if your EE wants it
  • A call to walk through it — reading it alone is not the plan
How a sprint runs
pre-tapeout review · rev C · 6-layer
Fail
Return path discontinuity — DDR4 byte lane 2

Twelve nets cross a plane split under U3. Adds ~180 ps of skew and a radiating loop right at the 240 MHz harmonic. Fix: reroute on L4 or add stitching caps within 3 mm of the crossing.

Fail
Radiated emissions margin — 240 MHz

5th harmonic of the 48 MHz clock lands on a cable resonance, +6.1 dB over FCC Class B at 3 m. Fix: common-mode choke on the USB shield plus a 22 Ω series terminator at the source.

Risk
Decoupling placement — U4 core rail

PDN impedance peaks at 1.9 Ω near 90 MHz, above your 0.8 Ω target. Fix: move C12 within 2 mm of U4 pin 8 and add one 100 nF on the back side.

Pass
PCIe Gen4 channel — all 4 lanes

Eye height 41 mV, width 0.62 UI at the receiver, both inside spec across the corner set. No action needed.

How an engagement runs

Four steps, and you know the price after the first one

No discovery-call maze, no rate card that turns into an open-ended hourly bill. You get a fixed number and a date before any work starts.

We are not a test lab. Formal FCC and CE certification has to happen at an accredited facility. We work alongside yours — the job here is to make sure that submission passes the first time, and to fix it fast if it does not.

STEP 01

Send the board and the deadline

Design files, stackup, schematic, and what you are afraid of. We come back inside one business day with a fixed price and a scope in writing.

STEP 02

We extract and solve

Full-wave 3D extraction and channel modelling on cloud compute we manage. No licenses for you to buy, no cluster for you to babysit.

STEP 03

You get a plain-English report

Pass / Risk / Fail per item, ranked by how likely it is to sink your submission, each with a specific change and the part number to make it with.

STEP 04

We re-check the fix

Send the revised layout and we re-run the affected checks. Confirming a mitigation is part of the sprint, not a second invoice.
