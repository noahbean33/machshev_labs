# My Background in Applied Mathematics

I've wanted to launch something like EpsilonForge for years: a software development studio, R&D lab, and consultancy in one. This is the right moment to do it.

I've spent my career as an applied mathematician, moving between problems and asking which applications were worth pursuing and which mathematics was worth building around them. I've been an industry intern, worked on technology transfer projects moving research from academia into government agencies, completed a PhD, done a post-doc, and worked as a full-time researcher. Between those environments, I've seen what mathematical R&D actually looks like from most of the angles there are to see it from.

What I took from that: spending most of my time in academia rather than inside a company with real customers has a ceiling. Academic work struggles to push past proof-of-concept. Early-stage ideation matters, but industry is where advances get tested against reality and where that pressure feeds back into stronger fundamental research. That feedback loop is what makes industry the more interesting place to build from.

That's why I wanted a different environment, one where I can put mathematical skill and software craftsmanship to work together instead of keeping them separate.

## Specialization and Trade: Software and R&D Services

EpsilonForge exists to provide specialized services and custom mathematical software to the global market.

The idea behind it is simple: specialization and trade have driven human progress for centuries, and modern information technology has opened more room for both than ever before. A small, specialized team can now reach a global market directly.

That doesn't mean working in isolation. Remote work and the gig economy make the model possible, but I want to stay connected to people doing original work in the field. I'm keeping active ties to my alma mater institutions, the University of Buenos Aires and CONICET, through joint R&D projects.

I see EpsilonForge as a bridge between global industry and local researchers and students, one that brings funding and motivation to promising research that might not otherwise get pursued.

If this resonates with you, stay tuned. I'll be sharing updates on the services I offer, what I'm exploring outside client work, joint projects with academic partners, and where I see the industry heading.
