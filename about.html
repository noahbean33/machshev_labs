About

Electromagnetics shouldn't cost $50,000 a year to get an answer

Machshev Labs exists to democratize computational electromagnetics for hardware startups — replacing six-figure enterprise licenses and PhD-level desktop tools with affordable, straightforward EMI/EMC, SI/PI, and FCC pre-compliance support.

The gap this fills

The physics has been solved for decades. What has not been solved is getting it into the hands of a three-person hardware team six weeks before tape-out.

The enterprise CEM vendors built for aerospace and semiconductor primes — organisations with dedicated EM specialists, HPC clusters, and procurement departments that can absorb a six-figure annual license. That is a real market, and those tools are good at what they do. They are simply not addressable if your entire engineering team fits in one room.

The alternative is a consultant or an EMC lab. Also real, also legitimate — but opaque on price, slow to quote, and pitched in language a founder without an RF background cannot evaluate. So the work gets deferred, and the first honest feedback anyone gets is a failed FCC submission with a respin attached.

Between those two is a gap, and a lot of avoidable pain sitting in it. That gap is the whole business.

Who this is for

  • Seed to Series B hardware companies
  • Consumer electronics, IoT, wearables, robotics, EV, drones, industrial, medical devices
  • One to three hardware engineers, no in-house EM specialist
  • Pre-compliance stage, with a first FCC submission on the calendar

Who it isn't for

  • Teams who already run HFSS or Sigrity in-house and have someone who enjoys it
  • Anyone who needs an accredited certificate rather than an answer — you need a lab, and we will point you at one
  • Programs where the failure mode is mechanical, thermal, or firmware, which is more often than people expect
How we work

Four commitments, including the awkward one

Plain English, always

The deliverable names the part, the coordinate, and the change. If a report needs a PhD to action, it is not finished.

Published prices

Rates and ranges are on the site. Fixed fee agreed before work starts, with the scope in writing.

One business day to a quote

Send files and a deadline, get a number and a start date back. No discovery-call maze before anyone will talk price.

We'll tell you when it's not us

If the problem is thermal, firmware, or genuinely needs a lab, you get told that instead of a proposal. It costs us a job and saves you a month.

Services now, software later

The long-term goal is self-serve: upload a board, get an automated pre-flight report. That is a three-year target, not a launch claim.

Fully automated verdicts on arbitrary board geometry, with no engineer in the loop, is the hardest unsolved problem in this field — it is most of what the incumbents still have not automated. The failure mode is not an embarrassing demo; it is a startup shipping ten thousand units on a false "Pass."

So the order is deliberate. Expert-led engagements first, because that is where trust and revenue actually get built. Each one produces a matched pair — what the model predicted and what the hardware did — and that accumulating dataset is what will eventually make an automated verdict defensible. The consulting is not a detour around the product; it is the training loop for it.
