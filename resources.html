Calculator 01

Trace impedance

Characteristic impedance and propagation delay for a microstrip or symmetric stripline, straight from your stackup numbers.

Useful for a sanity check before you send the stackup to your fab, or when the impedance-controlled quote comes back and you want to know whether the geometry they proposed actually lands where you asked.

  • Copper thickness matters more than people expect — 0.5 oz is 0.7 mil, 1 oz is 1.4 mil
  • For microstrip, solder mask typically pulls Z₀ down by 1–2 Ω
  • A real field solver will disagree by a few percent; this is a first pass

Calculator 02

Plane resonance

Where your power and ground planes will resonate as a cavity — and therefore where PDN impedance spikes and edge radiation get worse.

If a clock harmonic or a switching frequency lands near one of these modes, you have a plausible suspect for both a PDN anti-resonance and a radiated emissions peak. It is one of the first things worth checking when a scan fails at a frequency nobody expected.

  • Compare the modes against your clock harmonics and SMPS switching frequency
  • Thinner plane separation raises capacitance without moving the resonances
  • Stitching vias around the board edge push the effective modes up and cut edge radiation
Calculator 03

FCC Part 15 limit converter

The radiated emissions limit that applies at your frequency, extrapolated to whatever distance you actually measured at — plus your margin and a Pass / Risk / Fail read.

Bench pre-compliance almost never happens at the standard's reference distance. This does the 20 dB/decade correction so you can compare your 1 m chamber-free measurement against a 3 m Class B limit without doing it on a napkin.

We treat anything under 6 dB of margin as RISK rather than PASS. Pre-compliance setups routinely differ from an accredited chamber by more than that, and a 2 dB pass on your bench is not a pass at the lab.
